Most of what I build starts as a problem I ran into at school and couldn't stop thinking about. A teacher losing an afternoon to attendance paperwork. ## Selected work

### NAEE — School Assessment and Execution Center

A full-stack exam proctoring system I designed and built on my own initiative, aimed at adoption by my school. Teachers author and schedule assessments from a web panel; students take them through a locked-down desktop client that restricts access to the rest of the machine during the exam. Programming questions are graded by running submitted code in an isolated execution environment.

The hard part was never the CRUD. It was deciding how much lockdown is proportionate, what happens when a student's machine dies mid-exam, and how to run untrusted code from a teenager without handing them the host.

### An iterative method for the area of a circular segment

I rediscovered a convergent iterative construction for circular segment area during a classroom problem, then worked backward to find out what I had actually found. It turned out to be a specific case of Archimedes' method of exhaustion. I formalized the derivation, proved convergence, and validated it numerically in GeoGebra and C++ against the closed-form result, with mentoring from one of my teachers.

Finding out that a 2,200-year-old mathematician got there first was, honestly, the best part.

### Digital attendance system

An attendance management system built for my school's coordination team, replacing a paper-and-spreadsheet workflow that consumed teacher time every single day. Designed around what the staff actually did, not around what would have been convenient to build.

### BuzzLine — line-following robot

An Arduino line follower with a PID controller. Most of the work was tuning: reading sensor noise honestly instead of filtering it until the graph looked nice, and learning that a controller that performs well on one track surface is not a controller that works.
